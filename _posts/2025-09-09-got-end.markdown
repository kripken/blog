When an important TV show nears its end, I like to write up how I would end it. Then I watch the final season or episode, and compare. Usually the show is much better! But, in my personal opinion, I prefer my own ending to Game of Thrones, and that is what is written up here.

Why am I writing this now? Game of Thrones came up randomly today in a conversation with my nephew and brother. I ended up in something of a rant, I'm afraid... Turns out the feelings from back then were only forgotten, not lost. So here they are.

I solemnly swear that the ideas below were written by me right after S8E1 aired in 2019, that is, after the first episode of the last season. I have edited and elaborated, but I have added nothing new. (That will seem hard to believe at one particular point, but it is true!)

## My 2019 Ending

Daenerys Targaryen and Jon Snow arrive in Winterfell.
  * Jon learns he is a Targaryen. "This changes nothing, I don't want the throne." But it does change some things:
    * He rides a dragon (see later)
    * Sansa is now clearly ruler of the North.

The Battle of Winterfell:
  * **Jamie Lannister dies saving Bran**: ironic as the show began with Jamie trying to kill him.
  * **The Night King dies by Arya backstab**. Yes, I swear I predicted the show on this! But, hear me out, for these are my reasons.
    * Arya was trained as a Faceless Man, a servant of the Many-Faced God and that god uses her as its agent now. **The Many-Faced God is the god of Death**, but importantly, death in the *natural* sense: a part of life, and sometimes necessary as the result of an assassination, but still, death in its proper place. On the other hand, **the White Walkers are a corruption of death**, the dead returning to life without limit and extinguishing the natural order. At Winterfell, *Life failed to stop Death, and so Death steps in*, using Arya, to restore the proper order.
  * With the Night King gone, **the Army of the Dead falls**, shattered and useless.
  * **But! Another White Walker raises all the newly dead** - plus the recently-dead of Stannis etc. from the Battle of the Bastards - and the battle is truly and utterly lost. **Winterfell falls. The North falls. The survivors flee south.**

The Battle at the Neck:
  * **The survivors regroup at the Neck, Moat Cailin, the last place where they can possibly stop the dead from overrunning all the rest of Westeros.** Their numbers grow with some forces that didn't reach Winterfell in time, and local forces from Riverrun.
  * **Jon + his dragon sacrifice themselves to slay the last of the White Walkers.**
  * **The Army of the Dead is finally defeated for good.**
    * Or is it? A mysterious figure is seen in the dark of night, going through the remains of the walkers and wights.

Politics:
  * A bittersweet victory, but short-lived: **Cersei Lannister approaches, bringing her own vast Westerosi armies plus the Golden Company.**
  * The battle is a close one, as Dany is loath to risk her one remaining dragon. But, finally, mad with grief after Jon's death, needing his sacrifice not to have been in vain, **Dany burns her enemies to ash**. Cersei flees and Dany + allies follow as soon as they are recovered.

King's Landing
  * The City's own defenses are not all Dany finds at the capitol: **There is also another Army of the Dead**, for Qyburn has continued "The Work" he began with undead Gregor Clegane, finally finishing it with samples from the Battle at the Neck. Cersei, through Qyburn, commands countless wights alongside her living troops, and can raise any that die on either side.
  * Dany intends to burn King's Landing to the ground - the only way to stop the undead once and for all - but Arya convinces her to delay, for the Many-Faced God once more uses her as its agent.
    * We see "Jamie" (who died at Winterfall saving Bran, recall) return to the city.
    * "Jamie" kills Cersei, repeating history, now as a queenslayer and not kingslayer. This is Arya using Jamie's face, who she took back them. She leaves the face as she does it, so Certi believes her brother killed her.
  * The Hound kills the undead Mountain.
  * Qyburn and his evil are ended by Melisandre, finishing her quest of Life against Death.
    * Melisandre succeeds because she arrives at the right place at the right time: **because she sees it in the flames**. But this time, she does *the opposite* of what she thinks her god wants. *She has realized that, as Davos told her, her god is evil* (and he was right; see Shireen). In fact, resurrection and prophecy have mostly been on the side of evil (a reversal of the usual fantasy tropes): All the resurrected men (Jon and Beric) achieved in the end was to give a dragon to the undead, which was the Walker's only way to destroy the wall. The Red God aka the Lord of Light was in fact the voice of the White Walkers: Death, not Life.
      * "In Winterfell I saw the truth. The Red God, we call our Lord, and R'hllor, but the more true name is the Lord of Light; the pale, colorless illumination that shines over the endless ice to the north of this continent. The color of snow, and of the White Walkers." - strongly hinting that what she thought were her gods communications were from the evil she was trying to fight.
      * Indeed, perhaps the prophecy warned of a dragon bringing down the Wall (talk of a horn might have been the horns on a dragon), and that was why dragons were never flown north of the Wall. But the White Walkers found a way to trick the Living and get them a dragon.
    * Qyburn's death death destroys all the remaining undead, and Melisandre burns whatever notes he wrote about the process of raising them.
  * Melisandre was aided by Arya here - again, the Many-Faced God works to quell the undead, to restore proper Death. But the Many-Faced God is not finished with Arya: she has taken many faces, too many, and becomes more and more "no one" - a pure agent of the god, and no longer a Stark girl. Arya thought she got away with all the powers but none of the costs, but that was not so.
    * Melisandre saves Arya - perhaps attempting to make up for killing another girl, Shireen. She holds Arya's face very close to her own - exactly she did in [that scene from season 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dp9xhOvhTFk&t=89s) - and calls Arya back to herself, back from the brink. "No more faces, girl!"


Epilogue
  * Tormund and Brienne marry. With Jaime dead, she is free to love again.
  * Arya sails beyond the western sea
  * Dany sits the Iron Throne, is pregnant with Jon's child(ren)
  * Tyrion is Hand of the Queen
  * Sansa is Wardeness of the North
  * Sandor refuses to be knighted
  * Final scene: Drogon has 3 eggs.

## My 2025 Thoughts

I think my ending is more of a **fantasy novel ending**, while the show ended up with more of a **big hollywood production ending**. The show had big battles and dramatic scenes, and works in the same way a superhero action movie works. Which is fine! But the show began as the best fantasy adaptation ever made, and ended up as something else.

What was the show missing? First, it left many questions opened. Not all should or need to be answered, but it often seemed completely uninterested in them at any level. I mean questions like
  * Who is the Red God, "the voice in the flames" we keep hearing about?
  * Why does Arya train with servants of the god of Death?
  * Why does Qyburn create an undead Gregor Clegane?

Etc. Now, "Because an undead Gregor Clegane is cool" is a fine answer for a superhero movie. But was all of that really not leading anywhere more? Was it not hinting something about Qyburn, who seemed incredibly fascinated by the wight that was brought down to King's Landing..?

Second, a satisfying ending needs to treat the main themes of the show with the proper weight. The undead are the very first scene we get, and they terrify us til the last season. But then, in the show, they are dispatched in the course of one episode (called by some more of a Short Evening than a Long Night). That felt far too easy. And Qyburn was right there! The perfect opportunity for someone to dabble in raising the dead, keeping this theme going til the very end.

Third, the ending should tie together the different themes and make sense of them. The White Walkers and Arya: Is there not a natural way those two fit? Death (the god behind Arya) vs undeath (White Walkers); death as a horrible but necessary part of existence, vs death corrupted to consume all. Those are natural enemies. We heard about both for seasons. This is why I put Arya as killing the Night King! I don't think it's a coincidence that I predicted that part right, and I would not be surprised to see this happen in GRRM's future books. The show, for its part, seems aware of the natural connection here - it chooses Arya correctly - but does nothing else. By making Arya have no downsides to her training, by not mentioning the god of the Faceless Men that trained her, and not touching on the importance of Death to her abilities, they basically left this topic alone. Sometimes it's good to keep things subtle, but if the rule is "show, not tell", I don't think they even showed the convergence and resolution of these themes.
