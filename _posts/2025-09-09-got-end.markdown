When an important TV show nears its end, I like to write up how I would end it. Then I watch the final season or episode, and compare. Usually the show is much better! But, in my personal opinion, I prefer my own ending to Game of Thrones, and that is what is written up here. I think my ending is more of a **fantasy novel ending**, while the show ended up with more of a **big hollywood production ending**.

Why am I writing this now? Game of Thrones came up randomly today in a conversation with my nephew and brother. I ended up in something of a rant, I'm afraid... Turns out the feelings from back then were only forgotten, not lost. So here it is.

I solemnly swear that the ideas below were written by me right after S8E1 aired in 2019, that is, after the first episode of the last season. I have edited and elaborated, but I have added nothing new. (That will seem hard to believe at one particular point, but it is true!)

## My 2019 Ending

Daenerys Targaryen and Jon Snow arrive in Winterfell.
  * Jon learns he is a Targaryen. "This changes nothing, I don't want the throne." But it does change some things:
    * Seems he+Dany would be a good king+queen.
    * He rides a dragon.
    * Sansa is now clearly ruler of the North.

Beric Dondarrion and Tormund Giantsbane race south, trying to arrive before the Army of the Dead with word that it broke through the Wall.
  * Beric sacrifices himself so that Tormund may make it, saying "I've been ready to die a long time"
    * Sandor later says of Beric, "he said we'd meet again" with sorrow.

The Battle of Winterfell:
  * Jamie Lannister dies saving Bran: ironic as the show began with Jamie trying to kill him.
  * Another dragon dies, leaving only Drogon.
  * The Night King dies by Arya backstab. Yes, I swear I more-or-less predicted the show on this! But, hear me out, for my reasons are wildly different:
    * Arya was trained as a Faceless Man, a servant of the Many-Faced God and that god uses her as its agent now. **The Many-Faced God is the god of Death**, but importantly, death in the *natural* sense: a part of life, and sometimes necessary as the result of an assassination, but still, death in its proper place. On the other hand, **the White Walkers are a corruption of death**, the dead returning to life without limit and extinguishing the natural order. *Life failed to stop Death, and so Death steps in*, using Arya, to return Death to its place.
  * With the Night King gone, the Army of the Dead falls, shattered and useless.
  * But! Another White Walker raises all the newly dead - plus the recently-dead of Stannis etc. from the Battle of the Bastards - and the battle is truly and utterly lost. Winterfell falls. The North falls. The survivors flee south.



The survivors regroup at the Neck, Moat Cailin - their last hope to keep the dead away from the rest of Westeros. They include now some late-coming forces from the south, and local forces.

  Jon dies to ensure victory.

  After the victory, we see Qyburn salvaging parts from the walkers and their wights, in secret.

With the dead finally defeated, the living find themselves threatened by Cersei's armies, now bolstered by the Golden Company, and already at the Twins and marching fast.

The battle looks to be an even one as Dany is loath to risk her one remaining dragon, keeps Drogon at safe tasks. But she is mad with grief after Jon's death, and end up bashing the enemy into burnt pulp.



They reach King's Landing, where it turns out Qyburn has continued "The Work" with remains of that zombie. Cersei now commands an army of the dead, and can raise any that die on either side.



Instead of destroying King's Landing as well, Arya convinces Dany to try another tack.

Later we see "Jamie" return to the city.

"Jamie" kills Cersei (Arya took his face way back when). Arya purposefully does not take off his face, or takes it off late, so she believes her brother killed her.

  This makes it a second time that Jamie/"Jamie" kills a monarch; both a king- and a queen-slayer.



The Hound kills the Mountain.



Qyburn and his evil are ended by Melisandre, finishing her quest of Life against Death. His death destroys all the remaining undead, and with him dies the knowledge of how to raise more.

  Melisandre succeeds because she arrives at the right place at the right time, because she sees it in the flames. But this time, she does *the opposite* of what she thinks her god wants. She has realized that, as Davos said, her god may not be good. In fact, resurrection and  prophecy have mostly been on the side of evil (a nice reversal of the usual fantasy tropes): all the resurrected men (Jon and Beric) achieved in the end was to give a dragon to the dead, which was the Walker's only way to destroy the wall. And Stannis burning Shireen etc. are more signs it's the god of death whose work she did, not life.



 Perhaps the prophecy was that only a dragon can bring down the wall. A horn to bring it down represents the horns on a dragon. And this explains why dragons were never flown north of the wall.

  Melisandre saves Arya while doing so (fitting, she slew one girl - Shireen - but saved another)

The Faceless Men's god, the god of death, is not done with Arya; she thought she got away with all the powers but none of the costs, but in truth she became that gods' agent against the Walkers and then the Walker's magic at King's Landing; she becomes more and more "no one". Melisandre brings her back from the brink: "No more faces, girl!" (completing their brief encounter from a few seasons back)


Epilogue

  Tormund and Brienne marry. With Jaime dead, she is free to love again.

  Arya sails beyond the western sea

  Dany sits the Iron Throne, is pregnant with Jon's child(ren)

  Tyrion is Hand of the Queen

  Sansa is Wardeness of the North

  Sandor refuses to be knighted

  Final scene: Drogon has 3 eggs.
