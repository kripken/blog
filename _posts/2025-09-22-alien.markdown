*(spoilers for* ***Alien: Earth*** *Season 1)*

When is a xenomorph *not* a xenomorph?

When you show it behaving like a loyal dog. Tame, docile, controlled.

The basic rules of an Alien movie are:

1. **The Alien is absolutely terrifying.**
2. **No really, the Alien is so terrifying that any attempts to harness and control its power fail horribly.**

From **Alien (1979)** to **Alien: Romulus (2024)** this recipe works: First, we get scared on a visceral level when we see the Alien slaughter characters we care about. Second, we learn that Evil Management is trying to use the Alien somehow, but it is just so wildly powerful that containment always fails. (Even the oddball **Alien vs Predator** movies repeat the theme: Aliens are too dangerous even for Predators to manage.)

Definitely looks like the Alien will be a terrifying threat to our planet! But **Alien: Earth** Season 1 seems to make every effort to downplay its danger:

* Lots of deaths, but **many episodes pass before the Alien kills a named character**.
* **We see the Alien in, um, embarrassing situations**: grabbed awkwardly by the tongue, later struggling as it is wrestled with.
* **The Alien becomes Wendy's loyal servant**: It *can* be harnessed and controlled. Easily, in fact: Evil Management were right all along!

So, what is going on here?

Perhaps **Alien: Earth** defies tradition very intentionally. Maybe the point of this prequel is to say, **yes, Evil Management were right. They had proof from the start that the Alien could be controlled**, ***and that is why they kept trying in all those other movies you've already seen.*** Kind of a neat new perspective to give us about all prior Alien content, but is it a satisfying one?

**Or is the point to build up the setting slowly, and Season 2 will show us xenomorphs slaughtering Wendy and everyone she cares about?** But Season 1 spent so much time underplaying the Alien that I'm curious how that would work. Zombie movies don't usually start by showing docile zombies.

In the end, this is a show with many good parts: Timothy Olyphant, the eye-monster, some perfectly-delivered lines like the one I paraphrase in the title. But, so far, **this is not a show about a terrifying Alien and the hubris of trying to tame it.** This is a show about the hybrid children, superbeings whose souls were transported from their dying bodies à la Black Mirror, in conflict with their "parents". And with some dangerous alien lifeforms in the mix (in fact, most of them more horrifying than the Alien).
